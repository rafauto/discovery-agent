# Interview 01 — Ana, receptionist at a dental clinic

_Fictional interview for demo purposes. Clinic with 4 dentists, ~60 appointments a day._

**Interviewer:** Thanks for joining, Ana. Can you walk me through the start of a typical day?

**Ana:** Sure. I get in at 7:30 and the first thing I do is open the agenda and start confirming tomorrow's patients on WhatsApp. One by one. I copy the name, the time, the dentist, paste it in a message and send it. On a busy day that's 60 messages and it takes me most of the morning, in between answering the phone.

**Interviewer:** What happens after you send them?

**Ana:** Half of them don't answer. So in the afternoon I call the ones who didn't reply. And even when they say yes, some still don't come. Yesterday we had four no-shows. Four empty chairs. Dr. Paulo gets really annoyed because he could have seen someone else.

**Interviewer:** When someone cancels, what do you do with the slot?

**Ana:** If they cancel with notice, I try to fill it. I have a notebook with names of people who asked for an earlier appointment. But it's a mess, I have to call each one and by the time someone says yes, the time has passed. Most of the time the slot just stays empty.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned the phone. How many calls do you get?

**Ana:** A lot. And most of them are people who want to change their appointment. "Can I move my Tuesday to Thursday?" I have to check the dentist's agenda, the room, and it takes forever while the line is ringing again. Patients get angry when they can't get through.

**Interviewer:** Anything else that worries you?

**Ana:** Double bookings. We have two systems, actually — the clinic software and the paper agenda for the orthodontist who only comes on Fridays. Last month I booked two patients at the same time with him because the paper one wasn't updated. It was very embarrassing, one of them had taken the day off work.

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing tomorrow, what would it be?

**Ana:** Not having to send all those confirmation messages by hand. It's the most boring part of my job and it doesn't even work that well.
