# Interview 04 — Carla, receptionist at a dermatology clinic

_Fictional interview for demo purposes. 3 dermatologists plus aesthetic procedures in two rooms._

**Interviewer:** Carla, what's the hardest part of managing the agenda here?

**Carla:** The procedures. A consultation is simple, 20 minutes with the doctor. But a laser session needs the doctor, a specific room and the machine, and the machine is shared between two rooms. The system only blocks the doctor's time. So I keep a separate spreadsheet for the machine. If I forget to update it, we have two patients and one machine.

**Interviewer:** Has that happened?

**Carla:** Twice this year. One patient waited an hour and left. She posted a bad review on Google. The owner was furious.

**Interviewer:** How do you handle confirmations?

**Carla:** WhatsApp, manually, the day before. For procedures it's even more important because there's preparation — the patient can't have sun exposure, has to stop some creams. I send those instructions by hand too, copying from a Word document. Sometimes I forget, and the patient arrives unprepared and we have to reschedule. The whole slot is lost.

**Interviewer:** And cancellations?

**Carla:** Our procedure slots are long, one or two hours. When one cancels it hurts a lot. I have a WhatsApp group with patients who want to be called if a slot opens, but I'm not supposed to use a group like that because of privacy. And whoever answers first gets it, it's chaos.

**Interviewer:** What do you think about patients booking online by themselves?

**Carla:** For consultations, yes, please. For procedures I'm afraid, because they'd book without the room or the machine, or without the evaluation they need first. It would have to understand our rules.
