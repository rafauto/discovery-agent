# Interview 02 — Marcos, owner of a physiotherapy clinic

_Fictional interview for demo purposes. 6 physiotherapists, two units in the same city._

**Interviewer:** Marcos, what keeps you up at night when it comes to the schedule?

**Marcos:** Empty slots. A physio session is 50 minutes. If a patient doesn't show up, that's 50 minutes I'm paying the professional for and getting nothing. I did a rough count once on a spreadsheet and we lose something like 15% of the agenda to no-shows and late cancellations. But honestly I don't know the real number.

**Interviewer:** Why don't you know?

**Marcos:** Because the system doesn't tell me. I can see the appointments, but there's no report of who didn't come, which professional has more empty slots, which day of the week is worse. I export to Excel at the end of the month and spend a Sunday trying to make sense of it. By then it's too late to do anything.

**Interviewer:** What would you do with that information?

**Marcos:** Plenty. If I knew that Monday mornings are always half empty, I'd run a promotion or move a professional to the other unit. If I knew a specific patient misses all the time, I'd ask for a deposit. Today I'm flying blind.

**Interviewer:** You have two units. How does scheduling work between them?

**Marcos:** That's another headache. Each unit has its own receptionist and its own agenda. A patient calls unit A, there's no slot, and the receptionist doesn't know that unit B has three free slots that afternoon. We lose the patient to a competitor because of that. It's absurd.

**Interviewer:** Have you tried anything to reduce no-shows?

**Marcos:** We tried charging a cancellation fee, but it was hard to enforce. The receptionist has to remember, call the patient, it creates conflict. In the end nobody charged it. And automatic reminders — our current software has an SMS add-on but it's expensive per message and the patients don't reply to SMS anyway. Everybody here uses WhatsApp.

**Interviewer:** How do your physios feel about the agenda?

**Marcos:** They complain that they find out about cancellations only when they're already waiting for the patient. If they knew earlier they could do paperwork or take another patient.
